## What is a database?
A database is an **organized collection of data** — the organization is what matters. It lets us find, update, manage and use data.
- See [[Relational Database]] for tables, rows and columns
- Example: a company with 10,000 customers (number, name, address, phone, email, order history) stored so it can be worked with efficiently

## Why databases?
Spreadsheets or paper work for 5 customers, not 5 million.
1. **Finding** — quickly answer "every customer who ordered in the last 30 days"
2. **Updating** — change an address once, not in five places
3. **Relationships** — customers place orders, orders contain products; a *relational* database stores the relationships, not just the data

Databases sit under almost everything: Amazon orders/search/cart/stock, Instagram, banking, Netflix, Uber, school registration.

## Database vs. DBMS
- **Database** — where the data is stored
- **DBMS** — the software to create, manage, access and work with it
- Analogy: database = the library's collection; DBMS = the system for cataloguing, searching, checking out and managing access

See [[Database vs DBMS]].
